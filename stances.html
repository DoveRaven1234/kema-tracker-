How We Got Here: Timeline of Flashpoints

The cases every delegate should be able to cite. Red dots = artist-side ruptures.

2009

TVXQ vs. SM - the "slave contract" case

Three members sue over a 13-year exclusive contract with punishing terms. The fallout pushes the KFTC to cap exclusive contracts at 7 years and issue standard contract templates - the legal foundation of everything since.

2014

EXO's Kris & Luhan file to nullify SM contracts

Foreign members head for the exit, citing health and unequal treatment - proof the 2009 reforms hadn't ended the underlying conflict, only rebranded it.

2017-2019

Jonghyun, Sulli, Goo Hara

Three deaths in two years force a mental-health reckoning over relentless schedules, cyberbullying, and image control. Agencies introduce counseling programs and "health hiatuses"; critics call the pressure structural, not incidental.

2022

Lee Seung-gi's 18 unpaid years

A top star reveals he never received music revenue from his agency across his entire career. Public fury converts directly into payment-transparency legislation - the clearest case of scandal becoming statute.

2022-23

Chuu vs. Blockberry · Omega X

Chuu is expelled from LOONA amid agency claims, fights back in court, and wins; Omega X members allege mistreatment on camera. Smaller agencies come under the spotlight the majors usually absorb.

2023

Fifty Fifty try to leave ATTRAKT at their peak

Weeks after "Cupid" goes global, the group seeks to suspend their contracts. The injunction fails, three members are dropped, and the case becomes the corporate side's favorite cautionary tale about third-party "tampering."

2024-25

NewJeans vs. ADOR/HYBE - the era-defining battle

The biggest new act of its generation publicly declares its contracts terminated over management and autonomy grievances. Courts side with ADOR and enforce the deal. Legally a corporate win; reputationally, the question "who owns an artist?" goes mainstream worldwide.

2026

The fight goes international: VCHA's KG sues JYP USA

A member of JYP's American-formed group files to terminate her contract alleging abuse and mistreatment - testing whether the K-pop management model survives contact with US labor norms. Ongoing: follow it in the daily article feed.
