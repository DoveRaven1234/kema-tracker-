Methodology

How This Works

What this site is

A daily research companion for the Jakarta MUN / KEMA committee topic "The Seoul of K-Pop: Balancing Corporate Competition with Creative Freedom." It compiles the top 30 current articles on the debate — 15 leaning toward the corporate model, 15 leaning toward artists' creative freedom — plus a curated recap of the major issues.

Where the articles come from

The For/Against pages pull from Google News RSS using four targeted searches (two angled at each side of the debate). Results are de-duplicated, scored, and ranked. The Press Room additionally pulls real RSS feeds directly from six named outlets — Soompi, Koreaboo, and NME in English; Yonhap, SBS, and Hankyoreh in Korean — with links going straight to the publisher. No API keys, accounts, or servers are required.

How Trends works

Once per day the tracker snapshots the coverage tilt, issue counts, and source diversity. Opening the site locally records the snapshot in your browser; the GitHub Action records it in data/history.json when deployed. The Trends page merges both, so the chart grows one point per day.

How "daily updates" work

  • Opened locally or on any static host: the first visit each day fetches fresh articles and caches them in your browser. Every later visit that day is instant; the next day it refreshes automatically. The ↻ Force refresh button clears the cache on demand.
  • Deployed on GitHub Pages (optional): the included GitHub Action (.github/workflows/daily-update.yml) re-generates data/articles.json every day at 06:00 WIB, so the site is fresh even before anyone visits.

How stance labels are assigned

Each article's title and summary are scored against two keyword lists — one for pro-corporate signals (growth, investment, revenue, expansion…) and one for creative-freedom signals (lawsuit, dispute, exploitation, mental health, artist rights…). The higher score wins; ties fall back to which search surfaced the article.

This is a heuristic, not a judgment. Automated stance detection is approximate: a news report about a lawsuit isn't necessarily advocating for the artist, and an earnings story isn't an editorial defending the system. Treat labels as a starting sort — always read the article before citing it in committee.

Ranking

Rank = stance-confidence × 2 + a freshness bonus (up to +7 for same-day articles). Strongly on-topic, recent coverage floats to the top.

Honest limitations

  • Google News links route through Google's redirect URLs.
  • Feeds are fetched through four free gateways tried in order (allorigins → rss2json → corsproxy.io → codetabs). If all are down, the site falls back to yesterday's cached edition, then to curated search links — it never shows a blank page.
  • Keyword classification can mislabel sarcasm, neutral reporting, and opinion pieces.
